StreamLine Spotlight

From a 15,000-title warehouse to a few picks viewers trust

StreamLine has 15,000 titles and flat engagement. Viewers see it as a warehouse of mid-tier content. Spotlight replaces the endless scroll with three explained picks a night, chosen by editors and matched to each viewer's taste, to win back the viewers who value quality over volume.

We must stop StreamLine's slide into a commoditized, mid-tier content warehouse by fixing the endless, low-trust browsing that leaves viewers scrolling for 20 minutes, falling back on comfort rewatches, or leaving for competitors that hand-pick for them. The argument in five lines

  1. Crisis: doing nothing locks in the "warehouse" label while quality-seekers move to curation-led rivals.
  2. Misery: viewers scroll 20 minutes and give up, rewatch comfort shows, trust friends over the algorithm, or cancel.
  3. Bet: a hybrid curation model. Editors choose a pool of standout titles; the algorithm only matches that pool to each viewer.
  4. Reach: in the app to retain subscribers, and by weekly email to win back lapsed ones.
  5. Prerequisite: fix watchlist sync and cross-device resume first (Phase 0), or every good pick gets lost between phone and TV.

02 / The problem

Viewers are lost at two different moments

Strategic crisis

If we do nothing for 12 months, StreamLine is locked into the "warehouse of mid-tier content" label. Engagement slides from a plateau into decline as quality-focused viewers move to curation-led rivals, leaving us competing on catalog size and price, a race we lose to bigger libraries.

Moment of misery

After 20 minutes of look-alike tiles, viewers close the app without watching (Priya went back to a DVD), rewatch the same few comfort shows (Aisha), trust friends over the algorithm (Sam), or cancel for a competitor that emails them hand-picked films (Tom).

Why this framing matters: it answers the first objection a stakeholder will raise ("why not just fix the bugs?"). The two problems lose viewers at different moments, so they need different fixes.

04 / Strategy

Editors choose, the algorithm matches

For discerning viewers who value quality over volume (and the overwhelmed evening viewers who share the same pain), we will replace the 15,000-title scroll with Spotlight: three standout picks a night from an editor-curated pool, matched to each viewer's taste and explained ("why this, why now"), in the app for subscribers and by weekly email for lapsed ones, because competitors are already winning with hand-picked curation, quality-seeking subscribers like Tom are leaving for it, and every month we wait the "warehouse" perception gets harder to reverse. VALUE PROPOSITION · problem-hook.md §5

Fewer, not more

Spotlight replaces the scroll instead of adding another "Top 10" row.

Explained

Every pick says why it fits this viewer, tonight. That's the trust layer rivals lack.

Deep catalog

Editors surface gems from 15,000 titles, which a two-film newsletter can't match.

05 / Future state

The Spotlight journey

  1. STAGE 1 · DISCOVER

    Action Opens app, sees 3 editor-picked titles matched to their taste → decides in seconds.

    State Relieved → trusts someone already filtered the warehouse.

    Fixes The "Because you watched" sequel loop.

  2. STAGE 2 · TRUST

    Action Reads the editor's note on a hidden gem → understands why it fits them.

    State Intrigued → feels seen as a person, not a genre.

    Fixes No need to ask friends or a competitor.

  3. STAGE 3 · COMMIT

    Action Taps Watch or saves to My List → title follows them to the TV.

    State Confident → the effort of choosing isn't wasted.

    Fixes Exact-title search and watchlists that vanish between devices.

  4. STAGE 4 · RETURN

    Action Finishes, saves or skips picks; next week's Spotlight adapts → taste sharpens.

    State Loyal → StreamLine is their trusted curator again.

    Fixes The reason to churn to a competitor's curated email.

06 / Rollout and risks

Fix the foundation, then curate

Risk a skeptic will raiseOur answer
"Why not just fix the bugs?"Bugs lose viewers after they choose; curation loses them before. The fixes become Phase 0 of Spotlight.
Human curation can't personalize at scaleHybrid: editors build a 100–200-title pool; the algorithm only matches it to each viewer.
The catalog may lack gemsPre-launch editor audit for 50+ titles, including restored older ones. If it fails, the problem is content, not discovery.
Lapsed viewers never open the appRetain in the app; win back by weekly email, the channel that already won Tom.
The discerning segment may be smallIt's the design persona; the larger Evening Viewer segment benefits from the same fewer, explained picks.
"Every streamer has a Top 10 row"Spotlight replaces the scroll, explains every pick, and draws on a deeper catalog than a newsletter.
Viewers won't rate picksLearn from passive signals (finished, abandoned, saved, skipped); ratings are optional.
